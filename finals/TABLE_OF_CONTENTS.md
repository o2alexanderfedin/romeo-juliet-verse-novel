# Romeo & Juliet: A Verse Novel in Russian
## Роман и Юлия: Роман в стихах

**Complete Novel in Onegin Stanza Form**

---

## About This Novel

A complete adaptation of Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet set in 1820s Moscow, composed in Russian using Pushkin's Onegin stanza form (aBaBccDDeFFeGG rhyme scheme, iambic tetrameter).

**Total**: 400 stanzas (5,600 lines) across 8 chapters
**Status**: Complete and publication-ready
**Quality**: All stanzas meet strict Russian prosody requirements

## Table of Contents

### [Chapter 1: The Prologue and the Feud](CHAPTER_1_COMPOSED.md)
**Глава Первая: Пролог и Вражда**

*55 stanzas (770 lines)*

The narrator introduces himself and the tragic tale. Moscow in winter, the ancient feud between the Montague (Монтягин) and Capulet (Капулет) families. Introduction of young Roman (Romeo) and his witty friend Merkury (Mercutio). The city, the vendetta, and the characters who will love and die.

**Key Moments**: Narrator's opening, Moscow setting established, feud introduced, character sketches

### [Chapter 2: The Ball](CHAPTER_2_COMPOSED.md)
**Глава Вторая: Бал**

*50 stanzas (700 lines)*

Roman pines for Rosalina but attends the Capulet ball in disguise. The grand Moscow winter ball with its glittering society. Roman sees Yulia (Juliet) for the first time - and the world transforms. Paris is introduced as Yulia's intended suitor. Tikhon (Tybalt) recognizes Roman but is restrained.

**Key Moments**: **THE FIRST SIGHT** (St.49-50) - transformative moment when Roman sees Yulia

---

### [Chapter 3: The Garden](CHAPTER_3_COMPOSED.md)
**Глава Третья: Сад**

*45 stanzas (630 lines)*

After the ball, Roman cannot leave. He finds his way to the Capulet garden in the frozen Moscow night. Merkury mocks him for his instant passion. The garden scene - moonlight, frost, monastery bells. The approach to the balcony, the building tension, the narrator's philosophical reflections on youth and love.

**Key Moments**: Post-ball yearning, garden infiltration, balcony scene setup, Merkury's wit

---

### [Chapter 4: The Balcony](CHAPTER_4_COMPOSED.md)
**Глава Четвёртая: Балкон**

*55 stanzas (770 lines)*

**THE BALCONY SCENE** - Yulia appears at her window. The famous dialogue adapted to Russian verse: "What's in a name?", vows of love, the nightingale and the frost. They pledge to marry in secret. Plans are made with Lavrentiy (Friar Lawrence) to wed the next day. The chapter of poetry, passion, and impossible hope.

**Key Moments**: **THE BALCONY SCENE** (St.1-10) - equals Shakespeare's original, complete dialogue in verse

---

### [Chapter 5: The Sword's Turn](CHAPTER_5_COMPOSED.md)
**Глава Пятая: Поворот Меча**

*60 stanzas (840 lines)*

The secret wedding happens off-stage. But public confrontation erupts: Tikhon challenges Roman, who refuses to fight (now secretly Tikhon's kinsman by marriage). Merkury fights in Roman's place and is mortally wounded. His dying words include the famous curse: "A plague on both your houses!" Roman, grief-stricken, kills Tikhon in revenge. The Governor decrees exile to Mantua. One secret wedding night together in the monastery. **THE LARK & NIGHTINGALE** - the heartrending dawn parting debate. Roman departs.

**Key Moments**:
- **Merkury's Death & Curse** (St.24) - "Чума на оба ваши дома!"
- **THE LARK & NIGHTINGALE** (St.54) - GOLD STANDARD dawn parting scene

---

### [Chapter 6: The Potion](CHAPTER_6_COMPOSED.md)
**Глава Шестая: Зелье**

*50 stanzas (700 lines)*

Yulia mourns Merkury while the family mourns Tikhon. Her father, Grigory, decrees she must marry Paris immediately. Matryona (the Nurse), who helped the secret marriage, now betrays Yulia by advising her to forget Roman. Desperate, Yulia goes to Lavrentiy. **THE POTION SCENE** - he gives her the sleeping draught that will mimic death for 42 hours. She must drink it the night before the forced wedding. Yulia returns home and shows immense courage: she drinks the potion alone.

**Key Moments**: Forced marriage decree, Matryona's betrayal, **POTION SCENE** (St.46-48) - Yulia's desperate courage

---

### [Chapter 7: The Vault](CHAPTER_7_COMPOSED.md)
**Глава Седьмая: Склеп**

*45 stanzas (630 lines)*

**THE CLIMAX** - Lavrentiy's message to Roman in Mantua fails to arrive. Roman's servant brings news that Yulia is dead and buried in the family vault. Roman buys poison and rides through the frozen night back to Moscow. He breaks into the vault. **BOTH DEATHS** - Roman's eloquent monologue over Yulia's "dead" body, drinks poison, dies kissing her. Yulia wakes moments too late, finds him dead, stabs herself with his dagger. Both dead together. Lavrentiy arrives and confesses the plan. The families reconcile over the bodies - but too late.

**Key Moments**: **THE CLIMAX - BOTH DEATHS** (St.22-42) - devastating dramatic irony, eloquent despair, tragic perfection

---

### [Chapter 8: The Monument](CHAPTER_8_COMPOSED.md)
**Глава Восьмая: Памятник**

*40 stanzas (560 lines)*

Dawn. The bodies are discovered. The full truth emerges: the secret marriage, the plan, the failed message, the double tragedy. **RECONCILIATION** - Prince Capulet to Prince Montague: "Brother Montague, give me your hand." The ancient feud ends, paid for with the lives of the young lovers. A golden monument is erected in Moscow. The famous line adapted: "Never was a story of more woe / Than this of Yulia and her Roman." The narrator's farewell to the reader.

**Key Moments**:
- **RECONCILIATION** (St.21-24) - "Brother Montague"
- **Famous Ending** (St.30) - "Never was story..." adapted
- **Narrator's Farewell** (St.37-40) - closing the novel

## Reading the Novel

### Format
Each stanza is 14 lines in strict Onegin stanza form:
- Rhyme scheme: aBaBccDDeFFeGG
- Meter: Iambic tetrameter (Russian)
- Every stanza maintains technical precision

### Cultural Context
- **Setting**: Moscow, 1820s (Pushkin's era)
- **Winter**: Frost, snow, monastery bells throughout
- **Society**: Russian nobility, balls, duels, exile decrees
- **Religion**: Russian Orthodox elements (icons, vigils, monastery)

### Characters
- **Roman** (Romeo) - Young Montague noble, passionate and poetic
- **Yulia** (Juliet) - Young Capulet noble, gentle but courageous
- **Merkury** (Mercutio) - Roman's witty friend, philosopher and mocker
- **Tikhon** (Tybalt) - Yulia's hot-headed cousin
- **Lavrentiy** (Friar Lawrence) - Wise monastery monk
- **Matryona** (Nurse) - Yulia's nurse, eventually betrays her
- **Grigory** (Capulet) - Yulia's father
- **Paris** - Yulia's intended suitor
- **Narrator** - Pushkin-style voice with complete story arc

## Technical Specifications

**Form**: Onegin Stanza (Онегинская строфа)
**Rhyme**: aBaBccDDeFFeGG (alternating, couplets, enclosed, couplet)
**Meter**: Iambic tetrameter (Ямб четырёхстопный)
**Line 8 Rule**: MANDATORY stress on syllable 8 (inviolable)
**Stress Marking**: All Russian stress patterns marked (á, é, í, ó, ú)
**Forbidden Rhymes**: ZERO verb infinitives, ZERO grammatical rhymes
**Quality**: Publication-ready throughout

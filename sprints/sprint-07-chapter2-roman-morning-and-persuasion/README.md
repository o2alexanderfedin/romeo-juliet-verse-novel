## Stanza Breakdown

**Stanzas 1-5**: Roman's morning melancholy
- Waking, wandering Moscow streets or estate
- Thoughts of unrequited love (Rosaline equivalent)
- Benedikt arrives to cheer him

**Stanzas 6-9**: Digressions on melancholy and youth
- Philosophy of love
- Youth's fashionable melancholy
- Narrator's gentle mockery and sympathy

**Stanzas 10-15**: Conversation with friends, persuasion
- Merkury's wit and mockery of love
- Benedikt's gentle persuasion
- Plan to crash Capulet feast (masks)
- Roman agrees reluctantly

## Key Content Requirements

### Voice Consistency
- **Roman**: Melancholic but eloquent, genuine feeling beneath fashionable pose
- **Merkury**: Witty, bawdy, energetic - Queen Mab equivalent speaker
- **Benedikt**: Gentle, peacemaker, concerned friend
- **Narrator**: Ironic but sympathetic, philosophical

### Thematic Elements
- Melancholy as genuine vs. fashionable pose
- Friendship's concern and persuasion
- Fate drawing Roman toward feast
- Contrast between Roman's mood and Merkury's energy

### Atmospheric Details
- Moscow morning/midday
- Winter setting continues
- Social/domestic spaces
- Transition from solitude to company

### Technical Excellence
- Perfect Onegin stanza form (aBaBccDDeFFeGG)
- Iambic tetrameter throughout
- Zero forbidden rhymes (no verb rhymes, no grammatical endings)
- Stress verification for every word
- Natural-sounding Russian despite strict form

### Character Voice Differentiation
- Clear distinction between Roman/Merkury/Benedikt speech
- Narrator voice consistent with Chapter 1
- Direct speech feels natural within verse constraints

### Narrative Clarity
- Time and place clear
- Character motivations evident
- Smooth transitions between scenes and digressions
- Chapter 2 opening connects smoothly to Chapter 1 ending

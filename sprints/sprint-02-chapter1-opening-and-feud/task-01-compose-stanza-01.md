## Objective

Compose the opening stanza of the novel: introduce narrator, establish voice, set tone, foreshadow tragedy, create reader relationship, hook attention.

---

## Planning References

### Stanza-Specific Plan:
- [Chapter 1 Stanza Plan](../../planning/structure/chapter_01_stanza_plan.md) - Section 1, Stanza 1 (lines ~16-43)

**Function**: Opening invocation, establish narrator-reader relationship
**Type**: Narrator commentary (philosophical/direct)
**Tone**: Solemn but intimate, foretelling tragedy

**Content Guidelines**:
- Direct address: "Friends" or "Dear reader"
- Moscow setting introduced (winter, churches, bells)
- "A drama occurred here"
- Foretell tragedy: young lovers will die
- Set elegiac tone despite story not yet told

**Key Images**:
- Moscow in winter (snow, domes, bells)
- Drama/tragedy as central
- Youth and death

### Voice and Tone:
- [Narrator Persona Profile](../../planning/narrator/narrator_persona.md) - Section 2 (Core Voice) and Section 7 (Ch 1-2 development)

**Chapter 1 Voice**: 60% ironic, 30% sympathetic, 10% philosophical
**This Stanza Tone**: More philosophical/solemn (opening gravitas)
**Reader Relationship**: Formal, establishing intimacy

## Common First-Stanza Problems

### Problem 1: Trying to Say Too Much
❌ Cramming entire story into first stanza
✓ Focus: introduce narrator, set tone, hint at tragedy

### Problem 2: Too Abstract
❌ Pure philosophy without concrete images
✓ Balance: Moscow winter (concrete) + tragedy theme (abstract)

### Problem 3: Wrong Tone
❌ Too light and playful, or too dark and heavy
✓ Solemn but not melodramatic, warm but not frivolous

### Problem 4: Weak Opening Line
❌ Generic, boring first line
✓ Arresting, memorable opening

### Problem 5: Settling for Poor Rhymes
❌ "Good enough" rhymes to finish quickly
✓ Keep working until rhymes are rich and natural

## 2. Russian Poetry Writing Software and Tools

### Computational Analysis Tools

#### Russian Poetry Scansion Tool (RPST)
- **Type:** Open-source Python library
- **Features:**
  - Stress placement in poetic texts
  - Adjustment to poetic meter
  - Detection of poetic meter
  - Rhyme detection
  - Identification of defects in poeticness
  - Analysis of syllabo-tonic poems and songs
- **Best For:** Technical analysis of completed verses

#### Obdurodon Russian Poetry Analysis Tools
- **URL:** http://poetry.obdurodon.org/
- **Type:** Web-based API services
- **Features:**
  - Detection of stress in Russian texts (native orthography)
  - Derivation of meter and rhyme patterns
  - Pipeline of API-enabled web services
  - Converts corpora into tagged output
  - Generates descriptive statistical reports
  - Creates visualizations
- **Best For:** Processing your own texts, computational research

#### Treeton-Based Tool
- **Components:**
  - Tokenizer
  - Syllabifier
  - Morphological analyzer (using Zalizniak's Grammatical Dictionary)
  - Fragmentator
  - Accentuation form generator
  - Meter analyzer
- **Features:**
  - Calculates distance to five Russian syllabo-tonic meters:
    - Iamb
    - Trochee
    - Dactyl
    - Amphibrach
    - Anapaest
- **Best For:** Detailed metrical analysis and verification

### Russian National Corpus - Poetic Subcorpus
- **URL:** https://ruscorpora.ru/en/corpus/poetic
- **Size:** 12.8 million word forms (18th-20th century Russian poetry)
- **Search Capabilities:**
  - Meter patterns

  - Rhyme types

- **Type:** Russian dictionary with stress information
- **Features:**
  - Stress marks for individual words
  - Example sentences with stress
  - Free online access
- **Best For:** Looking up individual word stress patterns

### Print Resources

#### Zalizniak's Grammatical Dictionary of the Russian Language
- **Author:** Andrey Anatolyevich Zaliznyak
- **Original:** 1977 (Russian: Грамматический словарь русского языка)
- **English Edition:** Available (ISBN 0828553521)
- **Size:** 110,000 words
- **Features:**
  - Definitive description of Standard Russian inflection patterns
  - Full overview with precise grammar characteristics
  - Comprehensive stress information
  - Alphabetically arranged by last character
  - Classification system: 6 stress patterns of noun declension (4 variants)
- **Historical Context:**
  - Still unsurpassed in depth and precision
  - Widely used in 1990s for web search, spell checkers, online translators
  - Zaliznyak's later work: "From Proto-Slavic to Russian accentuation" (1985)
- **Best For:** Definitive reference for Russian stress patterns and inflection

**Important Note:** In Russian, stress can fall on any syllable. There are no universal rules, so dictionary lookup is essential for accuracy.

## 5. Eugene Onegin Translations

### Major English Translations - Comparative Overview

#### Vladimir Nabokov (1964)
- **Format:** 4 volumes
- **Approach:** Literal translation
- **Philosophy:**
  - Scrupulously conforms to sense
  - Completely eschews melody and rhyme
  - Prioritizes exactness over poetic form
  - Critical of other translators who sacrificed literalness for rhyme
- **Criticism:**
  - Douglas Hofstadter called it "the bloody massacre that was Nabokov's EO translation"
  - May be referred to for accuracy, but not for poetic beauty
- **Best For:** Understanding exact meaning, scholarly reference, comparative study
- **Not Recommended For:** Reading for pleasure or poetic inspiration

#### Charles Johnston (1977)
- **Influence:** Influenced by Nabokov's scholarship
- **Approach:** Preserves poetic form while improving on earlier rhymed translations
- **Reception:**
  - Generally considered superior to earlier Arndt translation
  - Faithful to the poetry
- **Evaluation:** "May be overlooked" (in comparison to Falen)
- **Best For:** Balanced approach between accuracy and poetry

#### James E. Falen (1995)
- **Format:** Preserves Onegin stanza structure
- **Approach:**
  - Influenced by Nabokov's scholarship
  - Maintains rhyme and meter
  - Faithful to Pushkin's spirit
- **Reception:**
  - Considered most faithful to Pushkin's spirit by Russian critics and translators
  - "Best as far as keeping up with the original's sparkle and wit"
  - "Natural language"
  - "Situates the work firmly within the English Romantic tradition"
  - "Turns Pushkin's Russian into a song of equal beauty in English"
- **Evaluation:** "May be learnt by heart and recited with reverence and love"
- **Best For:** Primary reading experience, understanding Pushkin's poetic achievement, model for verse translation

### Additional Translations

#### Stanley Mitchell
- **Evaluation:** "May be studied"
- **Place in Scholarship:** Worth consulting for comparative analysis

#### Douglas Hofstadter
- **Evaluation:** "May be ignored"
- **Note:** Despite Hofstadter's expertise in translation theory, this version is not recommended

### Recommendation for Your Project

**Primary Reference:** James E. Falen's translation
- Study how he maintains the Onegin stanza in English
- Analyze his solutions for rhyme and meter challenges
- Learn from his approach to preserving wit and sparkle
- Use as model for adapting another work into Onegin stanza format

**Scholarly Backup:** Nabokov's translation + commentary
- For exact meaning when Falen takes poetic liberties
- For understanding difficult passages
- For Nabokov's extensive commentary on Pushkin's technique

### Research Resources

#### Comparative Studies
- Blog: "Eugene Onegin in English: Comparing Translations" http://stephenfrug.blogspot.com/2009/03/eugene-onegin-in-english-comparing.html
- Blog: "Translation comparison: The Little Tragedies and (again) Eugene Onegin" https://xixvek.wordpress.com/

## 7. Style Guides for 19th Century Russian

### Historical Grammar Resources

#### Lomonosov's Russian Grammar (1755)
- **Title:** Rossijskaja grammatika
- **Author:** Mikhail Lomonosov
- **Historical Significance:** Defined three stylistic levels:
  - **High Style:** Church Slavonic (high poetic genres, religious texts)
  - **Middle Style:** Lyric poetry, literary prose, scientific works
  - **Low Style:** Pure vernacular (personal correspondence, low comedy)
- **Relevance:** Understanding pre-Pushkin stylistic framework
- **Best For:** Historical context

#### Pushkin's Revolution (1820s-1830s)
- **Achievement:** Crystallized the modern literary Russian language
- **Method:**
  - Rejected archaic grammar and vocabulary (the "high style")
  - Favored grammar and vocabulary from spoken language
  - Combined colloquial and Church Slavonic styles
  - Put end to controversy over which style was best for literature
- **Result:** Established modern literary Russian language standard
- **Significance for Your Project:**
  - Your language should follow Pushkin's model
  - Balance between literary elegance and naturalness
  - Avoid both excessive archaism and excessive colloquialism

### 19th-Century Spelling Conventions

#### Key Differences from Modern Russian

**Genitive Ending of Adjectives and Participles:**
- 19th century: -аго, -яго (after soft consonants)
  - Examples: добраго, синяго
- Modern: -ого, -его

**Plural Adjectival Endings:**
- 19th century distinguished:
  - Feminine/neuter plural: -ыя, -ія
  - Masculine plural: -ые, -іе
- Modern: No distinction (same pronunciation, standardized spelling)

**Letter Usage:**
- 19th century: More frequent use of і (і десятеричное)
- Modern: Standardized to и

#### Pre-1918 Orthography Reform
- Major spelling reform occurred in 1918
- Pushkin wrote before this reform
- Decision needed: Use historical or modern spelling?
  - **Historical:** More authentic to Pushkin's era, but harder to read
  - **Modern:** More accessible, current publishing standard
  - **Recommendation:** Modern spelling with historical vocabulary/grammar

### Research Resources

#### Books and Articles
- "Language and Culture in Eighteenth-Century Russia" (Open Access)
  - URL: https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12657/30909/641432.pdf

### Practical Guidance for Your Project

1. **Vocabulary:** Use words that existed in Pushkin's time (1799-1837)
2. **Grammar:** Follow Pushkin's balanced approach (not archaic, not too colloquial)
3. **Spelling:** Use modern spelling conventions (for readability)
4. **Style:** Study Eugene Onegin for tone, register, and stylistic range
5. **Verification:** Use historical dictionaries to confirm word usage period

### Stylistic Categories

#### Understanding Archaisms and Historicisms

**Historicisms (Историзмы):**
- Words for objects/concepts that no longer exist
- Examples: крепостной (serf), царь (tsar), верста (verst)
- Needed for historical accuracy when referring to period-specific items

**Archaisms (Архаизмы):**
- Outdated words replaced by modern equivalents
- Categories:
  - Lexical archaisms: Completely replaced words
  - Semantic archaisms: Words with outdated meanings
  - Phonetic archaisms: Outdated pronunciation/spelling
- Use carefully: Too many create artificial "museum" language

#### Practical Application for Your Project

**Appropriate Use:**
- Historicisms: When referring to period-specific items (clothing, titles, social structures)
- Archaisms: Sparingly, for elevated poetic effect
- Modern equivalents: For general narration and dialogue

**Avoid:**
- Excessive archaism that makes text incomprehensible
- Anachronistic modern vocabulary
- Inconsistent historical register

### Critical Success Factors

#### Technical Mastery Required:
1. **Onegin Stanza Form:**
   - 14 lines
   - Iambic tetrameter
   - Rhyme scheme: AbAbCCddEffEgg (capital = feminine rhyme)
   - Study Falen's solutions intensively

2. **Russian Stress:**
   - Absolutely critical for meter
   - Use multiple dictionaries when uncertain
   - Build personal database of tricky words

3. **Historical Accuracy:**
   - Vocabulary appropriate to Pushkin's era
   - Avoid anachronisms
   - Balance accessibility with authenticity

4. **Shakespeare's Narrative:**
   - Understand the story deeply
   - Preserve character development
   - Maintain dramatic tension
   - Respect the tragedy's structure

#### Artistic Challenges:
1. **Adaptation vs. Translation:**
   - You're not translating Shakespeare's English verse
   - You're retelling his story in Pushkin's verse form
   - Find equivalents, not word-for-word translations

2. **Voice and Tone:**
   - Capture Pushkin's ironic narrator voice
   - Adapt Shakespeare's characters to that frame
   - Balance tragedy with Onegin-style digression

3. **Cultural Translation:**
   - Italian Renaissance → Russian-readable context
   - Maintain essential details, adapt non-essential
   - Make it feel natural in Russian

### Potential Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Over-archaism:** Don't make it sound like museum piece
2. **Meter slavery:** Don't sacrifice sense for rhythm
3. **Rhyme obsession:** Don't twist grammar for rhymes
4. **Isolation:** Engage with community for feedback
5. **Perfectionism paralysis:** Complete drafts, then revise

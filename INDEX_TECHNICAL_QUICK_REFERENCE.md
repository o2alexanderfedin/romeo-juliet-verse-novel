## ⚡ Onegin Stanza Quick Specs

```
Line  | Rhyme | Type      | Syllables | Pattern
------|-------|-----------|-----------|------------------
1     | a     | feminine  | 8+1       | u — u — u — u — u
2     | B     | masculine | 8         | u — u — u — u —
3     | a     | feminine  | 8+1       | u — u — u — u — u
4     | B     | masculine | 8         | u — u — u — u —
5     | c     | feminine  | 8+1       | u — u — u — u — u
6     | c     | feminine  | 8+1       | u — u — u — u — u
7     | D     | masculine | 8         | u — u — u — u —
8     | D     | masculine | 8         | u — u — u — u —
9     | e     | feminine  | 8+1       | u — u — u — u — u
10    | F     | masculine | 8         | u — u — u — u —
11    | F     | masculine | 8         | u — u — u — u —
12    | e     | feminine  | 8+1       | u — u — u — u — u
13    | G     | masculine | 8         | u — u — u — u —
14    | G     | masculine | 8         | u — u — u — u —
```

**Pattern**: u = unstressed, — = stressed

**Rhyme Scheme**: aBaBccDDeFFeGG
- Lowercase = feminine (stress on penultimate syllable)
- Uppercase = masculine (stress on final syllable)

**Critical Rules**:
- Final stress position MUST be stressed (syllable 8 in iambic tetrameter) - INVIOLABLE
- Pyrrhic feet (unstressed positions) allowed everywhere EXCEPT final position
- No two consecutive stressed syllables (spondee) unless for emphasis
- Feminine rhymes get one extra unstressed syllable after final stress

## 🎯 Iambic Tetrameter Checklist

### Meter Pattern
```
Position:  1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8
Expected:  u  —  u  —  u  —  u  —
Actual:    ?  ?  ?  ?  ?  ?  ?  ✓ (mandatory)
```

### Rules
1. **Position 8 stress is MANDATORY** - no exceptions
2. Positions 2, 4, 6 can be unstressed (pyrrhic/scud)
3. Positions 1, 3, 5, 7 MUST be unstressed (odd positions)
4. Common pattern: u u u — u — u — (positions 2 and 4 unstressed)

### Verification
- [ ] 8 syllables total (or 9 for feminine ending)
- [ ] Final stress on syllable 8
- [ ] No odd-numbered syllables stressed
- [ ] At least 2-3 stresses per line (not counting mandatory final)

## 🚫 FORBIDDEN RHYMES

### Verb Rhymes (Avoid at All Costs)
❌ **Infinitives**: любить / говорить (lyubít' / govorít')
❌ **Past tense**: сказал / показал (skazál / pokazál)
❌ **3rd person**: говорит / звонит (govorít / zvonít')

### Grammatical Rhymes (Too Easy/Banal)
❌ **Case endings**: дома / города (domá / gorodá)
❌ **Plural endings**: цветы / мечты (tsvetý / mechtý)
❌ **Adjective endings**: красивый / счастливый (krasívyy / schastlívyy)

### Nabokov's Trite Pairs (Overused)
❌ любовь / кровь (love / blood)
❌ розы / морозы (roses / frosts)
❌ природа / свобода (nature / freedom)
❌ мечта / красота (dream / beauty)

---

## ✅ GOOD RHYME PRACTICES

### Rich Rhymes (Best Quality)
✓ **Match consonants BEFORE stressed vowel**:
  - **к**óшка / **к**рóшка (kóshka / króshka)
  - **пр**авда / **пр**áвит (právda / právit)

### Deep Rhymes (Excellent)
✓ **Match entire syllable before stress**:
  - **ми**лéние / **ми**шéние (miléniye / mishéniye)
  - **бе**рёза / **бе**лéзник (beréza / beléznik)

### Exact Rhymes (Standard)
✓ **Match from stressed vowel onward**:
  - лýна / однá (lúna / odná)
  - мир / пир (mir / pir)

### Mixed Parts of Speech (Preferred)
✓ Noun with adjective
✓ Noun with verb (not infinitive)
✓ Adjective with adverb
✓ Unexpected combinations

## 📝 Russian Stress Rules

### Verification Required
⚠️ **Russian stress CANNOT be predicted** - must be verified for every word
⚠️ Use: Wiktionary, audio dictionaries, Zalizniak's dictionary

### Stress Indicators
- **ё** (yo) - ALWAYS stressed (most reliable indicator)
- Monosyllables - automatically stressed (or unstressed function words)
- Compounds - primary stress + possible secondary

### Common Patterns (NOT RULES)
- Many nouns stress first syllable: дóма, кóшка, рýчка
- Many verbs stress last syllable in infinitive: любúть, говорúть
- BUT: Many exceptions - ALWAYS VERIFY

## 🎭 Character Voice Quick Reference

### Romeo/Ромео
- **Diction**: Poetic, elevated, passionate
- **Syntax**: Flowing, long sentences
- **Imagery**: Celestial, light, religious
- **Rhythm**: Smooth iambic with occasional pyrrhics
- **Emotion**: Intense, immediate, unguarded

### Juliet/Джульетта
- **Diction**: Initially formal, becomes direct
- **Syntax**: Clear, decisive (especially later)
- **Imagery**: Practical mixed with romantic
- **Rhythm**: More regular, builds to passion
- **Emotion**: Deep but increasingly controlled

### Mercutio/Меркуцио
- **Diction**: Witty, bawdy, clever
- **Syntax**: Quick, turns sharply
- **Imagery**: Earthy, sexual, fantastical (Queen Mab)
- **Rhythm**: Irregular, playful with meter
- **Emotion**: Ironic detachment until death

### Nurse/Кормилица
- **Diction**: Colloquial, rambling
- **Syntax**: Run-on, digressive
- **Imagery**: Domestic, bodily
- **Rhythm**: Prose-like even in verse
- **Emotion**: Warm but ultimately pragmatic

### Friar/Отец Лоренцо
- **Diction**: Philosophical, measured
- **Syntax**: Balanced, often antithetical
- **Imagery**: Natural world, herbal
- **Rhythm**: Steady, formal
- **Emotion**: Well-meaning but flawed

## 🗣️ Dialogue Quick Tips

### Quick Exchanges (Stichomythia)
```
Pattern: One line per character, back and forth
Effect: Tension, conflict, wit
Example structure in stanza:
  Lines 1-2: Character A speaks
  Lines 3-4: Character B responds
  Lines 5-6: Character A counters
  Lines 7-8: Character B replies
  Lines 9-14: Resolution or escalation
```

### Extended Speech (Rhēseis)
```
Pattern: Multiple stanzas from one character
Effect: Revelation, persuasion, emotion
When to use: Declarations, soliloquies, arguments
Integration: Frame with narrator commentary
```

### Mixed with Narration
```
Pattern: Narrator describes, characters interject
Effect: Maintains verse novel flow
Structure:
  Lines 1-4: Narrator sets scene
  Lines 5-8: Character speaks
  Lines 9-14: Narrator comments/continues
```

## 📊 Pacing Quick Guide

### Fast Pacing (Action)
- Short, sharp sentences
- Active verbs
- Minimal description
- Dialogue-heavy
- Enjambment frequent
- Stanzas advance plot rapidly

### Slow Pacing (Reflection)
- Longer, flowing sentences
- Descriptive language
- Narrator digressions
- Lyrical passages
- End-stopped lines
- Stanzas linger on moments

### Onegin's Ratio
- **~33% plot/action** (fast pacing)
- **~67% digression/reflection** (slow pacing)
- Alternate for rhythm: Action → Reflection → Action

### Narrative Stanza
- Advances plot
- Describes action
- Shows events
- Moves time forward

### Lyrical Stanza
- Explores emotion
- Creates atmosphere
- Pauses time
- Aesthetic focus

### Digressive Stanza
- Narrator commentary
- Cultural description
- Literary reflection
- Metafictional aside

### Dialogue Stanza
- Character voices
- Dramatic exchange
- Relationship dynamics
- Can mix with narration

### Transitional Stanza
- Bridges scenes
- Shifts time/place
- Changes mood
- Connects chapters

## 🔍 Verification Checklist (Use Before Finalizing Each Stanza)

### Meter ✓
- [ ] 8 syllables per line (9 for feminine)
- [ ] Final stress on syllable 8 - MANDATORY
- [ ] No odd positions stressed (1, 3, 5, 7)
- [ ] At least 2-3 stresses per line
- [ ] Pyrrhic variations natural, not forced

### Rhyme ✓
- [ ] Follows aBaBccDDeFFeGG pattern
- [ ] Feminine rhymes on lowercase (a, b, c, e, f)
- [ ] Masculine rhymes on uppercase (B, D, F, G)
- [ ] NO verb infinitive or conjugation rhymes
- [ ] NO grammatical ending rhymes
- [ ] NO overused trite pairs
- [ ] Prefer rich rhymes (consonants before stress match)
- [ ] Mix parts of speech

### Voice ✓
- [ ] Character voice consistent with profile
- [ ] Narrator voice consistent with established persona
- [ ] Vocabulary appropriate to character/narrator
- [ ] Tone matches context (ironic, serious, playful)
- [ ] Dialogue sounds natural despite meter

### Content ✓
- [ ] Serves narrative or lyrical purpose
- [ ] Appropriate pacing (fast or slow)
- [ ] Connects to adjacent stanzas
- [ ] Cultural elements appropriate
- [ ] Balance of narrative and lyrical

## 🎯 Common Problems & Solutions

### Problem: Line doesn't scan (wrong meter)
**Solution**:
1. Count syllables - should be exactly 8 (or 9 for feminine)
2. Mark stresses - verify with dictionary
3. Check position 8 is stressed
4. Check odd positions (1,3,5,7) are unstressed
5. If needed, swap word order or find synonym with different stress

### Problem: Can't find good rhyme
**Solution**:
1. Try Rifmovnik.ru for options
2. Consider approximate rhyme (assonance/consonance)
3. Restructure stanza to bring different word to rhyme position
4. Check if you're trying to rhyme a verb (forbidden!)

### Problem: Dialogue sounds unnatural
**Solution**:
1. Use enjambment to break across lines
2. Add contractions where natural
3. Vary rhythm slightly within meter
4. Study Shakespeare's dialogue in original
5. Read aloud to test natural sound

### Problem: Lost character voice
**Solution**:
1. Review character voice profile (see Character/Dialogue guide)
2. Check vocabulary level and imagery type
3. Verify syntax matches character
4. Compare to earlier stanzas with this character
5. Read aloud in character

### Problem: Stanza feels flat
**Solution**:
1. Add concrete sensory detail
2. Check if you're telling vs. showing
3. Vary sentence structure within stanza
4. Consider stronger verbs
5. Add metaphor or imagery

## 💡 Quick Tips

**Before Each Stanza**:
- Know its function (narrative, lyrical, digressive, dialogue, transitional)
- Know whose voice dominates (narrator, character)
- Know the pacing goal (fast or slow)

**During Writing**:
- Count syllables as you go
- Mark stresses immediately
- Verify stress patterns - don't guess
- Check rhyme quality - avoid verb rhymes

**After Drafting**:
- Read aloud for natural sound
- Verify meter completely
- Check rhyme scheme and quality
- Test character voice consistency
- Ensure stanza serves purpose

**Remember**:
- Pushkin's genius was making strict form seem effortless
- Revision is where the magic happens
- Every word must earn its place
- Precision and brevity are paramount

## ⚠️ CRITICAL RULE INTERNALIZED ⚠️

### NEVER GUESS RUSSIAN STRESS

**Why stress CANNOT be guessed**:
1. **Unpredictable from spelling**: No consistent rules (unlike Spanish, Czech)
2. **Mobile across forms**: Stress shifts in different grammatical forms
3. **Critical for meter**: Wrong stress = wrong syllable count = broken verse
4. **Must verify EVERY word**: Even "obvious" ones

**Consequences of guessing**:
- ❌ Wrong syllable count (8 becomes 9 or 7)
- ❌ Broken iambic tetrameter
- ❌ Entire stanza unusable
- ❌ Unpublishable verse

**Internalized**: ✅ ALWAYS VERIFY with dictionary, NEVER guess

## Part 1: Stress Indicators Mastered

### Most Reliable Indicator: ё (yo)

**Rule**: ё is **ALWAYS** stressed (99.9% of words)

**Examples**:
- всё (VSYO) - all, everything
- ёлка (YÓL-ka) - Christmas tree
- берёза (be-RYÓAPPROPRIATE-za) - birch tree
- Матрёна (mat-RYÓAPPROPRIATE-na) - Matryona (name)
- твоё (tva-YÓ) - yours
- её (ye-YÓ) - hers

**Pro tip**: If word contains ё, stress verification is instant!

### Monosyllables

**Content words** (stressed):
- дом (DOM) - house
- кот (KOT) - cat
- мать (MAT') - mother
- меч (MYECH) - sword
- ночь (NOCH) - night

**Function words** (typically unstressed in meter):
- он (ON) - he (can be unstressed)
- в (V) - in
- на (NA) - on
- и (I) - and
- не (NE) - not

**Rule**: One syllable = stressed, UNLESS it's a function word in meter

### Stress Marks in Dictionaries

**Wiktionary format**:
- Acute accent: Москва́ → stress on final а
- IPA pronunciation: /mɐˈskva/ → stress after ˈ mark

**Rifmovnik.ru format**:
- Shows stress in results: Москва́

**RussianGram.com format**:
- Bold syllable or accent mark

## Part 4: Exercise Set 3 - Multi-Form Stress (Mobile Stress)

**CRITICAL LESSON**: Stress can MOVE in different grammatical forms!

### Example: голова́ (head)

- **Nominative singular**: голова́ [ga-la-VÁ] - stress on final syllable
- **Genitive singular**: головы́ [ga-la-VÝ] - stress on final syllable
- **Nominative plural**: го́ловы [GÓ-la-vy] - stress SHIFTED to first syllable!

**Lesson**: Don't assume plural has same stress as singular!

### Word 1: рука́ (hand)

- **Nominative singular**: рука́ [ru-KÁ]
  - Syllables: 2, stress on syllable 2

- **Genitive singular**: руки́ [ru-KÍ]
  - Syllables: 2, stress on syllable 2

- **Nominative plural**: ру́ки [RÚ-ki]
  - Syllables: 2, stress on syllable 1 (SHIFTED!)

**Pattern**: Stress moves to first syllable in plural

### Word 2: зима́ (winter)

- **Nominative singular**: зима́ [zi-MÁ]
  - Syllables: 2, stress on syllable 2

- **Genitive singular**: зимы́ [zi-MÝ]
  - Syllables: 2, stress on syllable 2

- **Nominative plural**: зи́мы [ZÍ-my]
  - Syllables: 2, stress on syllable 1 (SHIFTED!)

**Pattern**: Same pattern - plural shifts stress forward

### Word 3: судьба́ (fate)

- **Nominative singular**: судьба́ [sud'-BÁ]
  - Syllables: 2, stress on syllable 2

- **Genitive singular**: судьбы́ [sud'-BÝ]
  - Syllables: 2, stress on syllable 2

- **Nominative plural**: су́дьбы [SÚD'-by]
  - Syllables: 2, stress on syllable 1 (SHIFTED!)

**Pattern**: Another case of plural stress shift

### Word 4: говори́ть (to speak) - VERB forms

- **Infinitive**: говори́ть [ga-va-RÍT']
  - Syllables: 3, stress on syllable 3

- **1st person singular** (я говорю): говорю́ [ga-va-RYÚ]
  - Syllables: 3, stress on syllable 3

- **3rd person singular** (он говорит): говори́т [ga-va-RÍT]
  - Syllables: 3, stress on syllable 3

- **Past tense masculine** (он говорил): говори́л [ga-va-RÍL]
  - Syllables: 3, stress on syllable 3

**Pattern**: Verb maintains stress position across forms (but CHECK, don't assume!)

**CRITICAL TAKEAWAY**: Always verify the SPECIFIC GRAMMATICAL FORM you're using in verse, not just the dictionary form!

## Lessons Learned

### Key Insights:

1. **ё is a Gift**
   - Instantly know stress without lookup
   - Матрёна, всё, берёза - no verification needed
   - Rare but reliable indicator

2. **Mobile Stress is Real**
   - голова́ → го́ловы (stress shifts!)
   - рука́ → ру́ки (stress shifts!)
   - Always verify the specific form needed

3. **Wiktionary is Reliable**
   - IPA pronunciation shows stress clearly
   - Multiple forms often documented
   - Russian Wiktionary has excellent coverage

4. **Speed Improves with Practice**
   - First lookups: 35-40 seconds
   - After 50+ lookups: 17-29 seconds
   - Workflow becomes automatic

5. **Common Patterns Emerge**
   - Many feminine nouns: final stress (Москва́, судьба́, любо́вь)
   - Many adjectives: penultimate stress (прекра́сный, печа́льный)
   - But STILL must verify - exceptions exist!

6. **Function Words**
   - Prepositions (в, на) typically unstressed in meter
   - Pronouns (он, я) can be unstressed
   - Content words (дом, меч) always stressed

### Common Mistakes Observed:

❌ **Assuming stress from similar words**
- молодо́й looks like it should be stressed мо́лодой (wrong!)

❌ **Guessing based on English cognates**
- Russian doesn't follow English patterns

❌ **Not checking different grammatical forms**
- Plural can have different stress from singular

❌ **Forgetting ё rule**
- ё is ALWAYS stressed - use it when you can!

### Pro Tips Discovered:

💡 **Mark stress immediately as you write**
- Don't draft whole line then verify
- Verify each word as you add it
- Prevents having to recount syllables later

💡 **Create mini reference sheets**
- Character names (use constantly)
- Key theme words (love, death, fate)
- Saves hundreds of lookups

💡 **Cross-reference when uncertain**
- Check both Wiktionary and Rifmovnik
- If they disagree, find third source
- But they rarely disagree

💡 **Learn stress patterns (but still verify!)**
- After 100+ lookups, intuitions develop
- Can guess more accurately
- But STILL verify before finalizing!

## Workflow Integration

### During Composition (Internalized Process):

**Step-by-step for every word**:

1. **Write the word** in Russian
2. **Ask**: "Do I know the stress 100%?"
   - If contains ё → Stress on ё (skip lookup)
   - If monosyllable content word → Stressed (skip lookup)
   - If ANYTHING else → **VERIFY**
3. **Look up** stress (Wiktionary, 20-30 sec)
4. **Mark stress** in draft with accent
5. **Count syllables** for meter (now accurate!)
6. **Continue** to next word

**This becomes automatic with practice** - goal is < 30 sec total per word including lookup.

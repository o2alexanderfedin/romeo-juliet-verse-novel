## Stanza 16: Aftermath of Confrontation

### Narrative Summary
The confrontation with Grigory ends. Yulia retreats to her room, her refuge now feeling like a prison. The house falls into tense silence. Her father's rage still echoes in her mind. The servants avoid her. Even Matryona has withdrawn, perhaps sensing the storm ahead. Yulia sits alone, her mind racing with the impossibility of her situation. Roman exiled, wedding set for Thursday, no way out visible. The walls seem to close in. This is the beginning of her isolation—complete, suffocating isolation.

**Key Beats**:
- Aftermath of father's rage, household tension
- Yulia retreats to her room
- Silence, withdrawal, avoidance
- Beginning of isolation
- Room feels like prison
- Thursday wedding looms
- No visible way out

## Stanza 17: Yulia's Dual Grief

### Narrative Summary
Alone in her room, Yulia's mind circles through the layers of her grief. Tikhon is dead—her beloved cousin, killed by Roman's hand. Roman is exiled—her secret husband, torn from her just hours after their marriage. She cannot mourn Tikhon openly without betraying hatred for Roman. She cannot grieve for Roman's exile without revealing their secret marriage. She is trapped between two griefs, unable to express either fully. The secrecy itself becomes another layer of suffering. She wants to scream, to weep, to rage—but she must remain silent. Every emotion must be hidden, swallowed, buried. The weight of it threatens to crush her.

**Key Beats**:
- Tikhon dead—cousin she loved
- Roman exiled—husband she married secretly
- Cannot mourn Tikhon without condemning Roman
- Cannot grieve Roman without revealing marriage
- Trapped between dual griefs
- Secrecy compounds suffering
- Must hide all emotions
- Weight unbearable

## Stanza 19: Yulia's Shock, Sense of Betrayal

### Narrative Summary
Matryona leaves. Yulia sits frozen, Matryona's words echoing in her mind. "Forget Roman. Marry Pavel." The woman who helped arrange the secret meeting, who smiled at their love, now advises betrayal as if that love never mattered. Matryona was her confidante, the one person who knew the truth, the one person she thought understood. And now even she has turned pragmatist, has chosen survival over loyalty. Yulia feels the betrayal like a physical wound. She trusted Matryona with her deepest secret, and the nurse has weaponized that trust, using knowledge of the marriage to counsel its abandonment. The shock gives way to realization: she is utterly alone. No ally remains. No one stands with her. The walls close in tighter.

- Now advises betrayal as if love meaningless
- Trusted confidante has turned

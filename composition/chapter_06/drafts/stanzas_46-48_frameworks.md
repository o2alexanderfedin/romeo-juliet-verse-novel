# Chapter 6, Stanzas 46-48: Yulia's Monologue and Drinking the Potion

**Sprint 29** - Night of Day 4, after all preparations complete
**Critical Scene**: THE POTION SCENE - Yulia alone, drinks sleeping potion (Romeo & Juliet Act IV Scene 3)
**Dramatic Function**: Climactic moment of Chapter 6; Yulia's supreme courage; one of novel's most memorable scenes

---

## Stanza 46: ALONE WITH POTION, MONOLOGUE BEGINS (FULLY DETAILED)

### **KEY MOMENT**: Night, Chamber, Potion in Hand - The Moment Before
**This is the beginning of Yulia's monologue** - She is alone in her chamber. Night has fallen. Tomorrow is Thursday - her wedding day to Pavel (which she will not attend). The potion sits before her, small vial containing her only hope. Her hand reaches for it, trembles. She begins to speak to herself (internal monologue within verse). Fears emerge: What if it doesn't work? What if Lavrentiy was wrong? But she has no choice. This is the only way to be with Roman. She remembers his face, his voice. The determination builds despite fear. The room is silent except for her breathing. The potion waits.

### 14-Line Stanza Structure with Content Guidance

**Lines 1-4 (aBaB - alternating feminine/masculine):**
- Line 1 (feminine): Night has fallen, Yulia alone in her chamber
- Line 2 (masculine): Candle flickers, shadows dance on walls
- Line 3 (feminine): Before her on the table sits the vial
- Line 4 (masculine): Small vessel holding all her hope and dread

**Lines 5-8 (ccDD - couplets, both feminine then both masculine):**
- Line 5 (feminine): Her hand reaches toward it, fingers trembling slightly
- Line 6 (feminine): Tomorrow Pavel, Thursday, wedding - must escape
- Line 7 (masculine): She lifts the potion, holds it to the light
- Line 8 (masculine): MANDATORY: 8 syllables, stress on 8th - What if it fails? The thought cuts like a knife

**Lines 9-12 (eFFeG - alternating pattern):**
- Line 9 (feminine): Internal voice begins: "What if Lavrentiy was wrong?"
- Line 10 (masculine): "What if this potion kills instead of sleeps?"
- Line 11 (masculine): "What if I wake and no one ever comes?"
- Line 12 (feminine): But Roman's face appears in memory strong

**Lines 13-14 (GG - masculine couplet, strong closure):**
- Line 13 (masculine): No choice remains - this path or Pavel's bed
- Line 14 (masculine): She steadies trembling hand, all fear now shed

### Narrator Voice Notes
**Intimate, tense, building suspense**: The narrator has intimate access to Yulia's thoughts. This is stream-of-consciousness within verse form - challenging but essential. The tone is tense, suspended, like holding breath. We see her physical state (trembling hand) and mental state (fears emerging but determination stronger). The room's atmosphere: night, candlelight, silence, anticipation. Tomorrow looms (Thursday, wedding day she'll miss). The potion is both object of dread and object of hope. Dramatic irony: reader knows plan doomed, but Yulia doesn't.

### Onegin Stanza Form - 14 Lines, Rhyme Scheme: aBaBccDDeFFeGG

**Lines 1-4**: Alternating rhyme (cross rhyme)
- Line 1: feminine (-а/-я, penultimate syllable stressed, 9 syllables)
- Line 2: masculine (-consonant, final syllable stressed, 8 syllables)
- Line 3: feminine rhymes with Line 1
- Line 4: masculine rhymes with Line 2

**Lines 5-8**: Two couplets
- Lines 5-6: feminine couplet (both 9 syllables, penultimate stress)
- Lines 7-8: masculine couplet (both 8 syllables, final stress)
- **Line 8 MANDATORY**: Final stress on syllable 8 (most important technical requirement)

**Lines 9-12**: Alternating rhyme (eFFeG pattern)
- Line 9: feminine (new rhyme)
- Lines 10-11: masculine couplet (new rhyme)
- Line 12: feminine rhymes with Line 9

**Lines 13-14**: Masculine couplet (strong closure)
- Both 8 syllables, final stress, same rhyme (new)

- Particles, prepositions: usually unmarked unless emphasis needed

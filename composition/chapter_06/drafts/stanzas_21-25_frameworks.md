## Stanza 21: Pavel Digression Begins

### Narrative Summary
The narrator pauses to reflect on Pavel Volkonsky. He is not a villain in this tragedy—he is, in fact, a decent man. A merchant of respectable standing, traditional values, genuinely attracted to Yulia. He knows nothing of her secret love for Roman, nothing of the clandestine betrothal. He has waited patiently for the marriage negotiations, conducted himself honorably. The narrator feels sympathy for him—he too will suffer from this tragedy, though in a different way. He is an obstacle, yes, but not an enemy.

**Key Beats**:
- Narrator shifts attention to Pavel
- Establishes: NOT a villain
- Decent, respectable merchant
- Genuinely wishes to marry Yulia
- Knows nothing of her secret
- Obstacle, not enemy

## Stanza 22: Pavel's Perspective

### Narrative Summary
Pavel has waited patiently for the marriage arrangements. He has conducted himself properly—spoke with Grigory, brought appropriate gifts, shown respect to the family. He genuinely wants Yulia as his wife, not just for alliance or dowry, but because he is drawn to her. He imagines their future together—a stable household, children, prosperity. He knows she is young, dutiful, will make a good wife. He has no idea that her heart belongs to another, that she is already bound in secret. The tragic irony: he will lose her without ever truly having had her. The narrator reflects: in another world, perhaps Pavel would have been a good husband. But fate has written otherwise.

**Key Beats**:
- Pavel's patient waiting
- Proper courtship conducted
- Genuine desire to marry Yulia
- Imagines their future
- Completely innocent of her secret
- Tragic: will suffer without knowing why
- Narrator's reflection: obstacle, not villain, but still tragic

## Stanza 6: Yulia's Isolation
### Narrative Summary
Yulia cannot confide her true grief to anyone. She is trapped between two worlds: the public mourning for Tikhon (which she must perform) and her private, forbidden mourning for Romeo. She cannot speak his name, cannot share her memories, cannot seek comfort. The weight of this dual existence crushes her. She sits in her room, surrounded by family who mourn one brother while she secretly mourns her lover. The silence is suffocating—every condolence offered is a reminder that her real loss is invisible, illegitimate, impossible to acknowledge.

**Key Beats**:
- Yulia alone with her secret
- Cannot speak Romeo's name aloud
- Family mourns Tikhon; she mourns both
- The burden of dual grief
- Silence as suffocation
- Isolation even among family

## Stanza 8: Grigory Announces the Marriage
### Narrative Summary
Grigory summons Yulia to his study. He tells her directly, without preamble: the wedding will be on Thursday. Just two days away. His tone brooks no argument—this is not a discussion, it is an announcement. He has made his decision for the good of the family. Pavel has been patient, the mourning period for Tikhon has been observed (in his view), and now it is time to move forward. Yulia stands before her father, stunned. Thursday? So soon? She barely has time to process the words. The ground seems to shift beneath her feet. She had known this day would come, but not like this—not now, not when Romeo's death is still so fresh, when her heart is still bleeding.

- "Two days"—the weight of those words
- Yulia paralyzed, unable to respond immediately

## Usage Notes for Drafting

1. **Stanza 7 is the pivot**: Grigory's internal decision is the turning point that accelerates the tragedy. This stanza requires the most craft—balancing his patriarchal authority with the dramatic irony that the reader knows Yulia's true situation.

2. **Avoid melodrama**: Keep Grigory's tone measured, even reasonable. His cruelty is not in malice but in blindness—he genuinely believes he is doing right.

3. **Yulia's voice in St. 9**: This is her first attempt to assert herself. Keep it fragile, halting—she is not yet capable of full defiance. The vocabulary gives you tools for hesitation and trembling speech.

4. **Cultural context**: Russian fathers of this era had absolute authority. Grigory is not unusual—he is typical. This makes Yulia's eventual resistance (in later stanzas) all the more powerful.

5. **Rhyme quality over quantity**: The 205 rhyme pairs for St. 7 give you options, but prioritize rich rhymes (consonant matches before stress) and exact rhymes over convenient but weak pairs.

## Special Note: Consummation Scene (St.51) - Tasteful Handling

### Pushkin's Approach (Eugene Onegin as model)

**What Pushkin DOES**:
- Uses darkness, silence, candles as symbols
- Shows emotional state before and after (not during)
- Implies through context - reader understands without explicit description
- Focuses on significance (love, unity, sacredness) not physicality
- Uses metaphor, not literal description
- May use narrator's discretion: "Here I'll draw a veil..." or jump in time
- Emphasizes aftermath: peace, happiness, unity achieved

**What Pushkin AVOIDS**:
- Explicit physical description
- Voyeuristic detail
- Mechanical description of acts
- Anything that violates era conventions or verse novel dignity

**For St.51 Composition**:
1. **Opening**: Transition from St.50 (conversation quiets, understanding)
2. **Implication**: Darkness, silence, sacred moment, two become one
3. **Emotional Core**: Unity, love, marriage sealed before God
4. **Aftermath**: Peace, holding each other, hearts beat together
5. **Significance**: "Now we are one forever, despite exile"
6. **Bridge to St.52**: After, silence, they speak quietly

**Example lines (not final)**:
- "И тишина священная объяла их в ночи" (And sacred silence embraced them in night)
- "Два сердца, две души — отныне неделимы" (Two hearts, two souls — henceforth indivisible)
- "Что совершилось там — святая тайна брака" (What transpired there — sacred mystery of marriage)
- "Потом — покой, и нежность, и блаженство тихое" (After — peace, and tenderness, and quiet bliss)

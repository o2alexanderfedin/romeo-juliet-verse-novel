## "PLAGUE ON BOTH HOUSES" (Stanza 24)

### 1. AUTHENTICITY Assessment

**Russian Naturalness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)

**Evidence**:
- Core phrase "Чума на оба ваши дома!" uses natural Russian syntax
- "Чума" (plague) is perfect cognate - powerful in Russian, not "borrowed" feeling
- Multiple metrical alternatives provided:
  - "Чума на оба ваши дома!" (chu-MÁ na Ó-ba VÁ-shi do-MÁ)
  - "Чума на ваши оба дома!" (better iambic: chu-MÁ na VÁ-shi Ó-ba do-MÁ)
  - "Чума на ваши два дома!" (simpler: chu-MÁ na VÁ-shi DVA do-MÁ)
- All three variants sound authentically Russian, not translated
- Vocabulary bank includes native Russian curse/plague vocabulary: мор (pestilence, archaic), зараза (infection), яд (poison)
- Framework acknowledges Russian cultural context of plague imagery

**Cultural Appropriateness**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (5/5)

**Evidence**:
- 19th-century Russian verse novel conventions respected
- Pushkin's style markers explicitly addressed:
  - Precision (every word earns place)
  - Concrete imagery grounding abstract themes
  - Balanced syntax for equal blame
  - Emotional restraint through specific detail
  - Effortless meter despite strict form
- Character voice section ensures Merkury maintains wit/insight even dying
- No anachronisms in vocabulary selection
- Tasteful handling of death scene (not gratuitous)

**Translation vs. Adaptation**: **ADAPTED** ✓

**Assessment**:

This is a **successful cultural adaptation**, not a translation. Key evidence:

1. **Language feels native**: "Чума на оба ваши дома" could exist independently in Russian literature without referencing Shakespeare
2. **Multiple authentic variants**: Framework doesn't slavishly stick to one "translation" but offers Russian alternatives
3. **Cultural grounding**: Plague imagery has independent power in Russian (plague of 1771 in Moscow, cholera epidemics, etc.)
4. **Poetic tradition**: Curse formulas fit Russian poetic tradition (Lermontov, Pushkin's own curses in "The Bronze Horseman")

**Verdict**: This passage will read as authentically Russian verse, not "Romeo & Juliet translated into Russian."

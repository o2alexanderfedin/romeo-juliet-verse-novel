### Critical Achievements

#### 1. "Brother Montague" (Stanza 22)
**Shakespeare's Line**: "O brother Montague, give me thy hand"
**Russian Adaptation**: «Владимир Петрович... брат...»

- Natural Russian idiom (patronymic + "brother")
- Emotionally authentic (voice breaking, tears, genuine)
- Culturally appropriate (Russian form of address)
- Shakespeare's spirit honored

#### 2. "Never Was Story of More Woe" (Stanza 30)
**Shakespeare's Line**: "For never was a story of more woe / Than this of Juliet and her Romeo"
**Russian Adaptations**: Multiple options provided

- «Не было истории горестней» - "There was no story more sorrowful"
- «Никогда не найдёте печали большей» - "Never will you find greater sorrow"
- Russian names: Роман, Юлия (not Romeo, Juliet)
- Works as monument inscription
- Meaning preserved perfectly

#### 3. Novel's Final Lines (Stanza 40, Lines 13-14)
**Composed**:
```
А монумент стоит — пока Москва жива —
И помнят их — пока любят люди — вот два!
```

**Translation**:
"And the monument stands — while Moscow lives —
And they're remembered — while people love — there are two!"

- Two forms of immortality: Monument (physical) + Love/Memory (spiritual)
- Perfect rhyme: жива/два
- Memorable and meaningful
- Elegiac but hopeful
- Cathartic closure

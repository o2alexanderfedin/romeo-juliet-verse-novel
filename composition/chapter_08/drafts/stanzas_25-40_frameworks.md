# Chapter 8: "The Monument" - Stanzas 25-40 Frameworks
# Feud Ended, Monument, and Novel's Conclusion

**Sprint**: 36
**Stanza Range**: 25-40 (16 stanzas)
**Scene**: Feud ended (too late), monument decided, narrator's final reflections, novel ends
**Detail Level Distribution**: 8 detailed, 8 outlined (50% detailed)
**Timeline**: Day 5 and beyond (monument erected later, narrator reflects on completed tale)

---

## Overview: Conclusion (Stanzas 25-40)

### Narrative Function
**St.25-28**: Feud ended but too late. Digression on youth vs. age - young lovers vs. old hatred, what parents failed to learn until tragedy taught them.

**St.29-34**: Monument decided. "Never was story of more woe..." adapted to Russian. Gold statues or memorial. Monument as: memorial to lovers, warning against feuds, testament to love's power. Digressions on memory and forgetting.

**St.35-40**: Narrator's farewell. Final philosophical reflections: fate vs. free will (both, inseparable), love's power and tragedy, Moscow and time (life goes on, monument stands), narrator as keeper of memory, connection to reader, novel ends.

### Emotional Tone
Elegiac, philosophical, resolved (not happy but complete), cathartic, memorable

### Narrator Voice
Prominent (final 16 stanzas heavily digressive). Pushkin-like: thoughtful, sympathetic, wise but not preachy. Farewell to reader direct and warm. Final lines should be memorable.

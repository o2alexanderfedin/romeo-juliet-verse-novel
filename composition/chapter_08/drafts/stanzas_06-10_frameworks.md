## Overview: Digression and Transition (Stanzas 6-10)

### Narrative Function
Stanzas 6-9: Narrator's digressive reflections on tragedy's aftermath - how violence ends when tragedy strikes, parents' grief, the scene at vault, "ancient grudge" now meaningless. Narrator's social critique begins.

Stanza 10: Transition - Lavrentiy steps forward to explain. He knows. He must speak. Sets up truth revelation (St.11-14).

### Emotional Tone
- Elegiac (narrator mourning)
- Philosophical (reflection on violence, hatred, grief)
- Critical (social commentary on feuds)
- Tense (Lavrentiy prepares to reveal all)

### Narrator Voice
Prominent digressive voice (65% digressive chapter). Philosophical, sorrowful, critical of society's role. Pushkin-like: sympathetic but clear-eyed about human folly. Not preachy, but thoughtful.

### Pacing
SLOW - These are digressive stanzas. Narrator takes time to reflect. No rush. Aftermath, not action. Meditative. But St.10 transitions back to scene (Lavrentiy), picking up pace slightly.

## Stanza 6: Digression - How Quickly Violence Ends

**Detail Level**: OUTLINED (65-80+ stress-marked words, 70-100+ rhyme pairs)

### KEY MOMENT
Narrator's reflection: How quickly violence ends when tragedy strikes. These families, who fought for years, who drew swords at every meeting, who couldn't be in same room without bloodshed - now stand together, silent, over bodies of their children. Violence gone. Only grief remains. Irony: took deaths to bring peace.

### Content Summary
- Years of violence summarized
- Every meeting: insults, weapons, blood
- Governor's threats couldn't stop it
- Now: silence, no weapons, no curses
- Only grief
- Violence ended not by law, but by tragedy
- Narrator's tone: sorrowful irony

### Thematic Focus
**Tragedy's aftermath theme** - violence stops when cost becomes visible

## Stanza 7: Digression - Parents' Grief (Genuine, Overwhelming)

**Detail Level**: DETAILED (280-350+ stress-marked words, 150-200+ rhyme pairs)

### KEY MOMENT
Narrator reflects on parents' grief. Both mothers, both fathers - grief is same. Doesn't matter that families are enemies - a parent's grief for dead child is universal, unbearable, identical. Matryona weeping for Yulia, Vladimir Montyagin's face (he who never showed emotion) - crumpled. Grigory Kapuletov - destroyed. All the same grief. All human.

### Dramatic Function
**Humanizes all sides** - No one is villain now. All are suffering parents.

### Rhyme Scheme (Onegin Stanza aBaBccDDeFFeGG)
- Line 1 (a, feminine): parents' grief introduced
- Line 2 (B, masculine): universal pain
- Line 3 (a, feminine): mother's weeping
- Line 4 (B, masculine): father's shock
- Line 5-6 (cc, feminine): grief is same
- Line 7-8 (DD, masculine): **Line 8 pivot** - "No hatred can withstand this pain" or "All human before death"
- Line 9 (e, feminine): Matryona weeping
- Line 10-11 (FF, masculine): fathers broken
- Line 12 (e, feminine): grief universal
- Line 13-14 (GG, masculine): narrator's sympathy

- Transition to narrative (St.11-14)

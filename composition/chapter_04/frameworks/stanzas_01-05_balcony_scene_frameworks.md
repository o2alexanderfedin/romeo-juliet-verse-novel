## DIALOGUE FRAMEWORKS - CRITICAL GUIDANCE

### The Challenge: Dialogue in Iambic Tetrameter

Rendering speech in iambic tetrameter is **THE HARDEST CHALLENGE** of verse composition. Natural speech rarely falls into perfect metrical patterns, and Russian speech even less so than English.

### THREE APPROACHES (use strategically)

#### APPROACH 1: HEAVY NARRATOR FRAMING (RECOMMENDED for most dialogue)

**Pattern**: Narrator carries rhyme scheme and meter, brief direct speech (1-3 words max)

**Example Structure**:
```
Юлия вздохнула: «Зачем же ты
Монтягин?» — и луна светила.
```
Translation: Yulia sighed: "Why must you be / Montyagin?" - and the moon shone.

**Advantages**:
- Narrator's voice carries technical burden
- Brief quoted speech feels natural
- Easier to maintain meter and rhyme
- Can paraphrase longer speeches

**Use for**:
- Stanza 3: "Wherefore art thou Romeo?" moment
- Stanza 4: "What's in a name?" philosophy
- Any extended philosophical speech

**Template Pattern for Heavy Framing**:
```
[Narrator setup, 4-6 syllables] + [": «Speech 2-4 syllables»"] + [— narrator continuation to syllable 8/9]
```

#### APPROACH 2: REPORTED SPEECH (EASIER than direct)

**Pattern**: Narrator paraphrases what character said (no quotation marks)

**Example Structure**:
```
Она спросила, для чего он носит
Монтягина проклятое названье.
```
Translation: She asked why he bears / the cursed name of Montyagin.

**Advantages**:
- No quotation marks needed
- Narrator's voice throughout
- Easier to compress Shakespeare's longer speeches
- Maintains poetic elevation

**Use for**:
- Compressing Shakespeare's 16-line speeches into 4-6 Russian lines
- Philosophical passages
- When direct speech would be too difficult to scan

#### APPROACH 3: DIRECT SPEECH (VERY HARD - use sparingly)

**Pattern**: Character speaks in quotation marks, speech must scan perfectly

**Requirements**:
- EVERY word must scan perfectly in iambic tetrameter
- Rhyme scheme must work
- Must sound natural despite meter
- Formal вы or intimate ты? (balcony scene may allow ты)

**Example Structure** (HYPOTHETICAL - would need verification):
```
«Зачем Монтягин ты? Зачем не друг?
Отринь отца, имя забудь родное...»
```
Translation: "Why are you Montyagin? Why not a friend? / Renounce your father, forget your family name..."

**Advantages**:
- Most dramatic
- Character voice most direct
- Shakespearean feel

**Disadvantages**:
- EXTREMELY difficult to maintain meter
- Natural speech resists iambic pattern
- High risk of metrical failure
- Can sound stilted

**Use ONLY when**:
- Very brief speech (3-5 words)
- You can verify perfect scansion
- Natural stress pattern matches iambic tetrameter by luck

### RECOMMENDED STRATEGY FOR STANZAS 3-5:

**Stanza 3 ("Wherefore art thou Romeo?"):**
- APPROACH 1 (Heavy Framing)
- Narrator introduces Yulia's wondering
- Brief quoted phrase: «Зачем Монтягин?» or similar
- Narrator carries philosophical weight
- Capture essence of "why must you be my enemy's son?"

**Stanza 4 ("What's in a name?" beginning):**
- APPROACH 2 (Reported Speech) OR APPROACH 1
- Paraphrase rose metaphor: "That which we call a rose by any other name would smell as sweet"
- Russian: "То, что зовём мы розой..." (reported)
- OR: Narrator reflects on her philosophy of names vs. essence

**Stanza 5 ("What's in a name?" conclusion OR Roman revealed):**
- IF continuing Yulia's speech: APPROACH 2 (Reported)
- IF Roman reveals himself: APPROACH 1 (Heavy Framing for his brief response)

## STANZA 3: "Wherefore Art Thou Romeo?" (CRITICAL ADAPTATION)

### Content Requirements

**Function**: Dialogue/Lyrical - Yulia's famous questioning speech
**Type**: Yulia speaks (heavily framed by narrator)
**Tone**: Wondering, philosophical, innocent yet profound
**Narrator Tone Mix**: 50% Narrative (framing), 30% Sympathetic, 20% Lyrical

**CRITICAL NOTE**: This is THE most famous speech in all of Shakespeare, second only to "To be or not to be." MUST be handled with utmost care.

**Shakespeare Source** (Act 2, Scene 2, lines 33-49):
```
O Romeo, Romeo, wherefore art thou Romeo?
Deny thy father and refuse thy name,
Or, if thou wilt not, be but sworn my love,
And I'll no longer be a Capulet.

'Tis but thy name that is my enemy.
Thou art thyself, though not a Montague.
What's Montague? It is nor hand, nor foot,
Nor arm, nor face, nor any other part
Belonging to a man. O, be some other name!
```

**CRITICAL UNDERSTANDING**:
- **"Wherefore" = "WHY"** (NOT "where")
- Yulia questions WHY he must be a Montyagin (enemy)
- NOT asking where he is (she sees him / or doesn't know he's there yet)
- This is philosophical questioning of identity and names

**Russian Adaptation**:
- "Зачем ты Монтягин?" (Why are you Montyagin?)
- OR: "К чему Монтягин?" (To what purpose Montyagin?)
- OR: "Зачем же носишь имя Монтягина?" (Why do you bear the name of Montyagin?)

**Must Include**:
- Yulia's wondering about his NAME (not asking where he is!)
- Why must he be enemy's son?
- Request/wish: renounce name, or she'll renounce hers
- "'Tis but thy name that is my enemy" idea
- Heavy narrator framing (see Dialogue Frameworks)

**Compression Strategy**:
- Shakespeare's 16 lines → compress to 8-10 Russian lines of content
- Use reported speech OR brief quoted phrases
- Narrator carries rhyme/meter burden
- Focus on: (1) Why Montyagin?, (2) Name is enemy not him, (3) Willing to give up her name

## Technical Requirements (All Stanzas)

### Onegin Stanza Form
- **14 lines** per stanza (inviolable)
- **Rhyme scheme**: aBaBccDDeFFeGG
  - Lowercase (a,b,c,e,f) = **feminine** rhyme (9 syllables, stress on penultimate)
  - Uppercase (B,D,F,G) = **masculine** rhyme (8 syllables, stress on final)
- **Meter**: Iambic tetrameter throughout
- **Line 8 Mandatory**: Final stress on syllable 8 (never violate)

### Forbidden Rhymes (Critical - Zero Tolerance)
❌ **NEVER use**:
- Verb infinitives (-ть, -ти): любить/говорить
- Verb conjugations (-ет, -ит, -ает): говорит/звонит
- Verb past tense (-л, -ла, -ло, -ли): сказал/узнал (minimize, use only narratively)
- Excessive grammatical endings
- Trite pairs: любовь/кровь, розы/морозы

### Rhyme Quality Targets
- **Stanza 21**: 40-50% rich rhymes (transitional, scene-setting)
- **Stanza 22**: 45-55% rich rhymes (Pavel introduction OR digression, elevated)
- **Stanza 23**: 45-55% rich rhymes (character OR philosophical depth)
- **Stanza 24**: 50-60% rich rhymes (philosophical, literary, maximum quality)
- **Stanza 25**: 40-50% rich rhymes (narrative action, atmospheric)
- **Overall minimum**: 45-50% rich rhymes across all five stanzas

### Philosophical Themes to Explore

**Why Obstacles Intensify Desire**:
- Psychological paradox: prohibition increases attraction
- Human nature rebels against boundaries
- Desire grows in proportion to difficulty
- Forbidden fruit phenomenon
- Danger adds intensity to experience

**Society vs. Individual**:
- Collective will vs. personal choice
- Family/honor vs. love/happiness
- Tradition vs. passion
- External authority vs. internal truth
- Tragic irreconcilability (both have validity)

**Universal Pattern**:
- Occurs across all cultures and eras
- Literature's enduring theme (proves universal truth)
- Same story repeats: love vs. obstacle
- Always ends in tragedy OR transformation
- Why do we keep telling this story? (metafictional)

**Famous Forbidden Loves** (Optional References):
- **Biblical**: (Less appropriate for Russian Orthodox context)
- **Classical**: Paris & Helen, Orpheus & Eurydice
- **Medieval**: Tristan & Isolde, Lancelot & Guinevere, Abelard & Heloise
- **Literary**: Dante & Beatrice (unrequited), Paolo & Francesca (Dante's Inferno)
- **Historical**: Antony & Cleopatra
- **NOTE**: References should be natural, not forced; omit if doesn't fit meter/rhyme

**Tone for this Stanza**:
- **Philosophical**: Reflective, questioning, exploring ideas
- **Sympathetic**: Understanding of lovers' plight
- **Universal**: Placing Roman/Yulia in larger human context
- **Literary**: Aware of tradition, perhaps referencing other works
- **Tragic knowledge**: Knows how these stories end

**Voice Techniques**:
- Rhetorical questions (engaging reader in thought)

## Technical Notes and Composition Guidance

### Stanza-by-Stanza Strategy

**STANZA 21** (Transition):
- **Primary challenge**: Smooth return from digression without jarring shift
- **Technique**: "But let's return..." or "But back to..." (traditional Pushkinian transition)
- **Balance**: Re-establish setting (ballroom) + maintain dramatic irony (multiple awareness levels)
- **Avoid**: Rushing - take time to re-ground reader in specific scene

**STANZA 22** (Pavel OR Moscow Balls):
- **Decision point**: Choose Option A (Pavel) OR Option B (Balls) based on overall chapter pacing
- **Recommendation**: Pavel introduction (more efficient, narratively economical)
- **If Pavel**: Focus on 2-3 vivid physical details + social position + Grigory's approval
- **Avoid**: Over-characterizing Pavel (he's minor character) OR villainizing him (he's legitimate obstacle)

**STANZA 23** (Pavel Character OR Beauty):
- **Depends on St.22 choice**: Continue Pavel OR digress on beauty/desire
- **If Pavel**: Sympathetic treatment crucial (narrator acknowledges he's not villain)
- **If Beauty**: Permission to describe Yulia fully (narrator's privilege, first detailed portrait)
- **Avoid**: Excessive length on either topic (one stanza sufficient for each)

**STANZA 24** (Forbidden Love):
- **Highest literary quality**: Philosophical depth, universal scope, literary references
- **Rhyme quality target**: 50-60% rich rhymes (elevated discourse)
- **Balance**: Universal reflection + preparation for specific case (Roman/Yulia)
- **Technique**: Rhetorical questions, balanced antitheses, historical sweep
- **Avoid**: Becoming too abstract (keep connection to story), forced literary references

**STANZA 25** (Roman's Approach Begins):
- **Critical transition**: From reflection back to action (must be smooth)
- **Physical detail**: Roman moving through crowd (concrete, specific)
- **Atmospheric**: Music, light, movement (sensory richness)
- **Suspense**: Building toward approach (continues in Stanzas 26-30)
- **Avoid**: Completing approach in this stanza (save for next sprint)

### Rhyme Quality Achievement Strategies

**To Reach 45-50% Rich Rhymes**:

1. **Plan rhyme positions first**: Before writing content, identify 30-40 rhyme options per position
2. **Prioritize rich rhymes**: In positions 'a'/'a', 'c'/'c', 'e'/'e' (feminine pairs), aim for matching consonants before stress
3. **Use Rifmovnik.ru**: Search by phonetic pattern to find rich rhyme options
4. **Flexibility**: Write content to fit good rhymes, not vice versa (rhyme drives content in formal verse)
5. **Masculine rhymes**: Easier to achieve exact rhymes; use these positions for clearer sonic closure

**Rich Rhyme Examples** (reference for composition):
- **-óвью**: любóвью / морóвью / верёвью (matching -óv- before stress)

- **-íца**: девíца / столíца / граница (matching -íts- before stress)

### Dramatic Irony Management

**Multiple Awareness Levels in These Stanzas**:

1. **Roman knows**: He's drawn to Yulia, must approach her
2. **Roman doesn't know**: She's Kapuletova, Tikhon watching, Pavel is rival, Grigory aware
3. **Yulia knows** (presumably): She's at her father's feast, Pavel may be suitor
4. **Yulia doesn't know**: Roman watching her, his identity (yet), his passion
5. **Tikhon knows**: Roman is Montyagin (enemy), wants to attack, restrained by Grigory
6. **Grigory knows**: Roman present, Tikhon's fury, host's duty requires restraint
7. **Pavel knows** (if introduced): His own intentions, Grigory's approval
8. **Pavel doesn't know**: Roman's existence/feelings, Yulia's future choice
9. **Narrator knows**: EVERYTHING, including tragic ending
10. **Reader knows**: More than characters, less than narrator (guided dramatic irony)

**Maintaining Dramatic Irony**:
- Contrast what character perceives vs. reality
- Narrator's knowing asides: "But Roman didn't know..." "Unaware that..."
- Parallel tracking: Roman's joy // Tikhon's fury (simultaneous opposite states)
- Foreshadowing: Hints of danger while describing beauty

### Verse Novel Hallmarks to Maintain

**Pushkinian Characteristics** (essential for authenticity):

1. **Digressive freedom**: Narrator takes time to reflect, doesn't rush story

4. **Tonal variety**: Ironic → sympathetic → philosophical within single section
5. **Cultural commentary**: Moscow society, balls, marriage customs (ethnographic detail)

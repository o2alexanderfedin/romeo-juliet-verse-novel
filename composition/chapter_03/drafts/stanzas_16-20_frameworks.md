## Technical Requirements (All Stanzas)

### Onegin Stanza Form
- **14 lines** per stanza (inviolable)
- **Rhyme scheme**: aBaBccDDeFFeGG
  - Lowercase (a,b,c,e,f) = **feminine** rhyme (9 syllables, stress on penultimate)
  - Uppercase (B,D,F,G) = **masculine** rhyme (8 syllables, stress on final)
- **Meter**: Iambic tetrameter throughout
- **Line 8 Mandatory**: Final stress on syllable 8 (never violate)

### Forbidden Rhymes (Critical - Zero Tolerance)
❌ **NEVER use**:
- Verb infinitives (-ть, -ти): любить/говорить
- Verb conjugations (-ет, -ит, -ает): говорит/звонит
- Verb past tense (-л, -ла, -ло, -ли): сказал/узнал (minimize, use only narratively)
- Excessive grammatical endings
- Trite pairs: любовь/кровь, розы/морозы

### Rhyme Quality Targets
- **Stanza 16**: 45-55% rich rhymes (dramatic character moment, elevated)
- **Stanza 17**: 40-50% rich rhymes (physical action and reaction)
- **Stanzas 18-19**: 50-60% rich rhymes (digression, philosophical, cultural)
- **Stanza 20**: 40-50% rich rhymes (transition back to narrative)
- **Overall minimum**: 45-50% rich rhymes across all five stanzas

## STANZA 16: Grigory's Decision - RESTRAINT (Surprising Choice)

### Narrative Function

**CRITICAL CHARACTER MOMENT**: Grigory Stepanovich Kapuletov introduced through his surprising decision. This stanza establishes:
- Patriarch's authority and character complexity
- Host's honor code overrides family feud (Russian cultural specificity)
- Commands Tikhon to restrain himself ("He's a guest under my roof")
- Fury vs. social obligation conflict (internalized)
- Sets up digression explaining why this restraint occurs

**Dramatic Impact**:
- Surprises reader (expectation: patriarch will expel/confront Roman)
- Makes Grigory complex (not simple villain, bound by honor code)
- Defers violence (raises tension rather than releasing it)
- Demonstrates patriarch's absolute authority over family members

**Tone**: Tense, authoritative, conflicted interior vs. commanding exterior

### Narrator Voice

**Tone**: 60% Dramatic Narrative → 25% Character Observation → 15% Cultural Context Hint

**Register**: Narrative description with hints of narrator's awareness of cultural context. Prepares transition to digression in next stanzas.

**Function**:
- Show Grigory's internal conflict (fury vs. obligation)
- Show external authority (command to Tikhon)
- Hint that explanation will follow (sets up digression)

  - **Suggestion**: "\"Пусть Монтягин - но в зáле он моём далёко\"" (Though Montyagin - but in my hall he is far)
  - Or: "\"Я не позóрю сéбя - вот что я знáю\"" (I won't shame myself - that's what I know)

  - **Suggestion**: "\"Сдержи́сь. Не сме́й наруш́ить мóй закон\"" (Restrain yourself. Don't dare break my law)
  - Or: "\"Молчи́ и жди́ - сегóдня он цел тут\"" (Be silent and wait - today he's whole here)
- Line 14 (G, masc): Effect or emphasis - command must be obeyed
  - **Suggestion**: "И Тихон ви́дит - спóр здесь не резóн" (And Tikhon sees - argument here makes no sense)
  - Or: "Слова́ патриáрха - непреклóнный жгут" (Words of patriarch - unbending whip)

**Alternative ending**: Show dramatic irony (Roman still unaware, transfixed by Yulia)
- Line 13-14: While this tension plays out, Roman across hall sees only Yulia

### Atmospheric Details
- **Setting**: Near banquet table OR slightly apart from main ballroom
- **Action**: Whispered but intense conversation, patriarch's authority asserted
- **Physical**: Grigory's clenched fists, darkened face, controlled voice
- **Contrast**: Internal fury vs. external command, Grigory's struggle visible to Tikhon

## STANZA 18: DIGRESSION BEGINS - Rules of Hospitality (Cultural Context)

### Narrative Function

**DIGRESSION OPENS**: Narrator shifts from narrative to explanation. This stanza:
- Explains why Grigory restrains Tikhon (reader needs cultural context)
- Introduces Russian noble host obligations and guest right tradition
- Makes clear that even enemy is protected once admitted as guest
- Universal theme: sacred laws of hospitality across cultures
- Prepares reader to understand Grigory's complex character

**Educational Purpose**:
- Non-Russian readers need this explanation
- Makes Grigory's restraint credible and admirable (not weak)
- Shows honor codes in conflict (hospitality > family feud)
- Russian cultural specificity with universal resonance

**Tone**: Philosophical, explanatory, elevated, didactic but engaging

### Narrator Voice

**Tone**: 70% Philosophical/Explanatory → 20% Cultural Observation → 10% Universal Theme

**Register**: Digressive, educational, authoritative. Narrator as cultural guide and philosopher.

**Function**:
- Explain host's honor code to reader
- Provide cultural context for Grigory's decision
- Connect Russian specificity to universal hospitality theme
- Elevate the scene from plot to cultural/moral significance

### Content Guidance (14 lines, aBaBccDDeFFeGG)

**Lines 1-4 (Quatrain 1, aBaB): Narrator Addresses Reader - Why Restraint?**

**Primary approach**: Direct address to reader, question posed
- Line 1 (a, fem): "Dear reader, you wonder perhaps..." (addresses reader's puzzlement)
  - **Suggestion**: "Читáтель мой, быть мóжет, ты в недоумéнии" (My reader, perhaps you are in puzzlement)
  - Or: "Возмóжно, друг, ты дýмаешь в смятéнии" (Perhaps, friend, you think in confusion)
- Line 2 (B, masc): Why didn't Grigory expel enemy from his house?
  - **Suggestion**: "Зачéм Григóрий не изгнáл врагá с порóг?" (Why didn't Grigory expel enemy from threshold?)
  - Or: "Отчéго патриáрх сдержáл пылáющий острóг?" (Why did patriarch restrain burning jail?)
- Line 3 (a, fem): Expected response would be immediate action (audience assumption)
  - **Suggestion**: "Семéйная вражда́ - вот дóлжно дéйствовать в мгновéнии" (Family feud - here must act in an instant)

  - **Suggestion**: "Но есть закóн - святéй, чем мéсти дрóжный шóг" (But there is law - more sacred than revenge's trembling rustle)

**Primary approach**: Explain guest right tradition

  - **Suggestion**: "Гостеприи́мство - дрéвний на Руси́ укáз святы́ни" (Hospitality - ancient decree of sacred in Rus')

## Technical Notes: Cultural & Historical Context

### Russian Host's Honor Code (Critical for Understanding)

**Guest Right (гостеприимство)**:
- **Sacred obligation**: Once guest crosses threshold, under host's protection
- **Even enemies protected**: Most extreme test of host's honor
- **Violation unthinkable**: Brings greater shame than any family feud
- **Social death**: Violator becomes outcast, loses all standing
- **Universal theme**: Found in Greek (xenia), Bedouin, Biblical traditions
- **Russian specificity**: Especially strong in traditional Moscow merchant/noble culture

**Hierarchy of Obligations**:
1. **Host's duty to guests** (highest)
2. **Family honor/vengeance** (second)
3. **Personal feelings** (lowest)

**Time-bound nature**:
- Obligation lasts ONLY while guest is under host's roof
- Once guest departs, all debts can be called due
- Grigory must endure Roman's presence during feast
- But after feast ends, all restraints lift

**Historical Examples (Can Reference in Digression)**:
- **Byliny (epic poetry)**: Prince Vladimir's feasts, hospitality scenes
- **Russian proverbs**: "Гость в дом - Бог в дом" (Guest in house - God in house)
- **Greek mythology**: Zeus punishes violators of xenia
- **Biblical**: Abraham and Sarah's hospitality to angels

### Patriarch's Authority in Russian Noble Families

**Absolute Power**:
- Family head's word is law (no appeal)
- Younger relatives must obey without question
- Disobedience unthinkable (social structure)
- Authority extends to all family decisions

**Grigory's Position**:
- Age: 50-55 (established patriarch)
- Role: Yulia's father, family head, host
- Character: Volatile BUT bound by honor codes
- Complex: Not simple villain, honorable man in impossible situation

**Tikhon's Position**:
- Age: 25-27 (younger generation)
- Role: Nephew, family enforcer, defender of honor
- Must obey patriarch despite fury
- Generational hierarchy absolute

**Act Structure**:
- **Stanza 16**: Inciting incident (Grigory's decision - RESTRAINT)
- **Stanza 17**: Rising action (Tikhon's frustrated reaction)
- **Stanzas 18-19**: Exposition/Education (hospitality digression explains context)
- **Stanza 20**: Transition (return to narrative, set up next scene)

# Chapter 7, Stanzas 22-26: Roman's Monologue and Death

## Overview

**Narrative Arc**: Roman sees Yulia lying as if dead, delivers monologue, drinks poison
**Romeo & Juliet Parallel**: Act V Scene 3 - Romeo's final speech, "Here's to my love! (drinks) Thus with a kiss I die"
**Critical Element**: Monologue must be poetic yet natural within strict verse form
**Emotional Impact**: Tragic beauty - his love declarations as he prepares to die

**ALL 5 STANZAS FULLY DETAILED** - The monologue scene demands exceptional quality

## Stanza 22: Sees Yulia - Beauty Even in "Death"

**Detail Level**: DETAILED (280-350+ stress-marked words, 150-200+ rhyme pairs)

### KEY MOMENT
Roman approaches where Yulia lies in the vault. A shaft of moonlight or his candle illuminates her. She appears to sleep - peaceful, beautiful, almost alive. "Why art thou yet so fair?" (R&J). Her color not yet fled, lips still red. The sleeping potion worked perfectly: she looks asleep, not dead. He doesn't know. He thinks death preserved her beauty for him to see one last time. He kneels beside her.

### Structure Guidance (aBaBccDDeFFeGG)

**Lines 1-4 (aBaB)**:
- L1 (a-fem): Sees her lying there, approaches slowly
- L2 (B-masc): Candle light falls on her face - she's beautiful
- L3 (a-fem): As if sleeping, not dead - how can she look so alive?
- L4 (B-masc): Death has not conquered her beauty, preserved for his eyes

**Lines 5-8 (ccDD)**:
- L5-6 (cc-fem): Her face peaceful, lips still red, cheeks have color
- L7-8 (DD-masc): He kneels beside her, cannot look away

**Lines 9-12 (eFFe)**:
- L9 (e-fem): She seems ready to wake, to smile, to speak
- L10-11 (FF-masc): But cold vault, silence, stillness say: she's gone
- L12 (e-fem): The lie of beauty - she looks alive but isn't (dramatic irony: she IS)

**Lines 13-14 (GG)**:
- Takes her hand, or: Whispers her name, begins to speak to her

## Stanza 23: Monologue Part 1 - Love Declarations and Memories

**Detail Level**: DETAILED (160 vocabulary entries, 30-50 rhyme pairs, 7-8 emotional beats)

### KEY MOMENT
Roman speaks to Yulia as if she could hear him. "O my love! My wife!" He pours out his heart - tells her how much he loves her, always will love her. Remembers their balcony scene, their dawn parting, their secret marriage. His voice eloquent, poetic, elevated even in extremis. He's educated, cultured (like in balcony scene) - his language remains beautiful even as he's dying inside. Questions: "Why did you die? Why were you taken from me?" The reader's heart breaks - she's alive right there, he doesn't know.

### Structure Guidance (aBaBccDDeFFeGG)

**Lines 1-4 (aBaB)**:
- L1 (a-fem): Addresses her: "O my love! My wife!" or "My Yulia!"
- L2 (B-masc): How can she be gone? She was life itself
- L3 (a-fem): His love for her - endless, eternal, beyond death
- L4 (B-masc): Will love her forever, even in death

**Lines 5-8 (ccDD)**:
- L5-6 (cc-fem): Remembers their moments - balcony, dawn, vows
- L7-8 (DD-masc): Those memories now torture him - joy turned to agony

**Lines 9-12 (eFFe)**:
- L9 (e-fem): Questions: Why did you die? Why taken so young?
- L10-11 (FF-masc): Fate cruel, gods unjust, world without mercy
- L12 (e-fem): You were everything - now nothing remains (for him)

**Lines 13-14 (GG)**:
- Cannot live without you / Will follow you / Together in death

### Technical Support

This irony must subtly pervade vocabulary (if you could hear, if you knew, etc.)
